## 1. The 10-slide investor deck outline

Investors see a lot of decks and spend only a few minutes on each one. This order answers their questions in the order they ask them. Each slide has **one job**.

| # | Slide | The question it must answer | What to show |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Title** | What is this company, in one line? | Name, logo and one sentence: what you do and for whom. |
| 2 | **Problem** | What pain exists, and who has it? | One clear customer, their pain, how they handle it today and why that falls short. |
| 3 | **Solution** | How do you fix that pain? | What you offer, in plain words. The main benefit, not every feature. |
| 4 | **Product** | What does it look like and how does it work? | Screenshots or photos and the 3 steps a customer goes through. |
| 5 | **Market** | How big can this get? | Who buys, how many of them there are and what they spend. Show your maths. |
| 6 | **Traction** | Is there proof people want it? | Real numbers only: users, revenue, growth, pilots, waitlist. Put a date on every number. |
| 7 | **Business model** | How do you make money? | Price, who pays and how often. Cost to win a customer and what one customer is worth, if you know them. |
| 8 | **Competition** | Who else solves this, and why will you win? | A simple table or a 2x2 chart. Name real options, including "they use a spreadsheet" or "they do nothing". |
| 9 | **Team** | Why are you the right people? | Founders with the one fact that matters for each, plus key hires or advisors. |
| 10 | **The ask** | What do you want, and what will it get done? | The amount you are raising, what it pays for (3 or 4 parts) and the milestone it gets you to. |

**Tip:** if traction is your strongest point, move it up to slide 3 or 4. Lead with your best proof.

### Headlines that say the point
Every slide title should say the point, not just the topic. The reader should get the story from the titles alone.

| Slide | Weak title | Strong title (Tidepost demo) |
|---|---|---|
| Problem | Problem | Small tour operators take bookings by phone and miss customers after hours |
| Solution | Our solution | Tidepost lets customers book and pay for a boat tour in 2 minutes, day or night |
| Market | Market size | [X] small tour operators in our first 3 countries, each paying [Y] a year for booking tools |
| Traction | Traction | [X] paying operators, up from [Y] in [month] |
| The ask | Investment | Raising [amount] to reach [milestone] in [number] months |

Fill the [brackets] with your own real numbers. Never round up or guess.

### Rules for the whole deck
1. **One idea per slide.** If a slide has two ideas, split it.
2. **10 slides in the main deck.** Put extra detail in an appendix at the end.
3. **Big text.** If it is hard to read on a phone screen, it is too small.
4. **A source and a date on every number.** Small text at the bottom of the slide is fine.
5. **Same fonts and colours on every slide.** Two fonts at most.
6. **Send it as a PDF** with a clear file name, for example `Tidepost-deck-2026-10.pdf`.

### Common mistakes
- **A problem with no person in it.** "The tourism market is broken" says nothing. Name who hurts and how.
- **Market size from the top down.** "If we get 1% of a huge market" is not a plan. Count real buyers instead.
- **A feature list on the solution slide.** Show the one main benefit first.
- **"We have no competitors."** There is always another option, even if it is a spreadsheet.
- **An ask with no number** or no milestone.
- **Too much text.** If you would read it out loud word for word, cut it.

### Fill-in outline (copy this)
```
1. Title:          [Company] helps [customer] [do what]
2. Problem:        [Customer] struggles with [pain]. Today they [workaround], which [why it falls short].
3. Solution:       [Company] lets them [main benefit].
4. Product:        Step 1 [ ], Step 2 [ ], Step 3 [ ]. Add screenshots.
5. Market:         [Number] buyers x [yearly spend] = [market size]. Source: [ ]
6. Traction:       [Real number] [users / revenue / pilots] as of [date].
7. Business model: [Price] per [month / order / user], paid by [who].
8. Competition:    [Option A], [Option B], [doing nothing]. We win because [reason].
9. Team:           [Name], [role], [one fact that matters]. Repeat for each founder.
10. The ask:       Raising [amount] for [use 1], [use 2], [use 3] to reach [milestone] by [date].
```

## 2. Insight headlines: 8 before and after examples

A **topic title** names the subject: "Sales update". An **insight headline** says the point as a full sentence: "Online sales grew in May, mostly from returning customers".

People often read only the titles. Insight headlines let them get your message even if they skip the rest of the slide.

### How to write one in 4 steps
1. Look at the slide and ask: **"So what?"**
2. Write the answer as **one full sentence with a verb**.
3. Add the **number or the reason**, if you have one.
4. Keep it **short**: 2 lines at most, about 15 words.

### 8 before and after examples
These are generic demo topics. Fill the [brackets] with your own real numbers.

| # | Before (topic title) | After (insight headline) | Why it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sales update | Online sales grew [X]% in [month], mostly from returning customers | Gives the result and the reason |
| 2 | Customer survey results | Customers like the product but say delivery is too slow | States the main finding, not just "results" |
| 3 | Website traffic | Most visitors come from phones, so we fix the mobile page first | Links the data to an action |
| 4 | Hiring plan | We need 2 support staff by [month] to answer customers within [X] hours | Says what, when and why |
| 5 | Budget | Moving [amount] from ads to email keeps costs flat and reaches past buyers | Shows the trade-off |
| 6 | Product roadmap | Online booking ships in [month]; payments follow in [month] | Gives the order and the dates |
| 7 | Competitor review | Other tools win on price; we win on how fast a new user can start | Makes the difference clear |
| 8 | Project status | The launch is on track, but testing is 1 week behind | Honest status that flags the risk |

### Fill-in patterns (copy these)
```
[Thing] went [up / down] by [amount] in [period] because [reason]
[Finding], so we should [action]
[Option A] beats [Option B] on [what matters most]
[Project] is [on track / late] because [reason]; next step is [action]
We need [what] by [date] to [result]
```

### The 30-second test
Read **only the titles** of your deck, in order, out loud. If they tell the whole story, your headlines work. If they sound like a list of topics, rewrite them.

### Common mistakes
- **One-word titles** like "Overview", "Results", "Analysis" or "Q3". They name a topic but say nothing.
- **A headline the slide does not prove.** If the chart shows something else, change the chart or the headline.
- **Too long.** Three lines is a paragraph, not a headline. Move detail into the slide.
- **Vague words** like "strong", "good" or "significant" when you have a real number. Use the number.
- **A question as a title** ("Why are sales down?"). Give the answer instead.

## 3. Chart chooser: which chart for which message

Pick the chart from your **message**, not from your data. First write the insight headline (see [section 2](#2-insight-headlines-8-before-and-after-examples)). Then pick the chart that proves it.

### The cheat sheet (8 rows)
Demo headlines use the made-up company Tidepost. Fill the [brackets] with your own real numbers.

| # | Your message is about | Example headline | Best chart | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Change over time** | Bookings grew every month since [month] | **Line chart.** Columns also work for up to about 8 time periods. | Pie chart |
| 2 | **Ranking or comparing items** | Sunset tours sell more than any other tour | **Bar chart**, sorted biggest to smallest. Use flat (horizontal) bars when the labels are long. | 3D bars, random order |
| 3 | **Parts of a whole** | Half of all bookings now come from phones | **One stacked bar**, or a pie or donut with 2 to 4 slices | A pie with many thin slices |
| 4 | **How a total builds up or breaks down** | Fuel and crew take [X]% of each ticket price | **Waterfall chart** | Several separate pies |
| 5 | **Plan vs actual, or before vs after** | Costs came in [X]% under plan | **Two bars side by side** (clustered bar) for each item | Two separate charts the reader must compare |
| 6 | **How two numbers move together** | Longer tours get higher review scores | **Scatter plot** (one dot per item) | A line joining dots that are not in time order |
| 7 | **Spread or range** | Most orders are between [X] and [Y] | **Histogram** (columns for each range, such as 0 to 20, 20 to 40) | Showing only the average |
| 8 | **One big number** | [X] paying operators as of [date] | **No chart.** Show the number large, with a short label and the date. | A chart with one bar |

### 7 rules that make any chart easier to read
1. **The headline says the point.** "Bookings grew every month", not "Bookings 2026".
2. **Highlight one thing.** Put the bar or line that matters in your brand colour. Make the rest grey.
3. **Label the data directly.** Put names next to lines and bars, so the reader does not need a legend.
4. **Bar and column charts start at zero.** Cutting the axis makes small changes look big.
5. **Remove clutter:** 3D effects, shadows, heavy gridlines, extra decimals.
6. **Round numbers.** Write 1.2M, not 1,203,456, unless the exact figure matters.
7. **Add a source and a date** in small text under the chart.

### Where to find each chart
| Chart | PowerPoint | Google Slides |
|---|---|---|
| Line, bar, column, pie | Insert > Chart | Insert > Chart |
| Stacked bar, clustered bar | Insert > Chart > Bar, then pick the stacked or clustered style | Insert > Chart > Bar, then open the linked Sheet and change the chart type |
| Scatter, histogram, waterfall | Insert > Chart (scatter is called X Y; histogram and waterfall need PowerPoint 2016 or newer) | Make it in Google Sheets (Insert > Chart > Chart type), then Insert > Chart > From Sheets |

### The 5-second test
Show the slide to someone for 5 seconds, then hide it. Ask: "What was the point?" If they cannot say it, cut detail or change the chart.

### Common mistakes
- **Choosing the chart first** and the message later.
- **Pie charts for change over time.** A pie shows one moment, not a trend.
- **Too many lines** on one chart. More than 4 and nobody can follow them. Highlight one, grey the rest, or split the chart.
- **Different colours for the same thing** on different slides. Keep "this year" the same colour everywhere.
- **A chart that does not match the headline.** If the headline says "costs fell", the chart must show costs falling.

## 4. Slide design checklist: 12 rules

Good design is not decoration. It helps people read the slide fast and trust it. These 12 rules work in PowerPoint and Google Slides, for any deck.

### The 12 rules
| # | Rule | What to do | Quick check |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **One idea per slide** | If a slide makes two points, split it into two slides. | Can you say the point in one sentence? |
| 2 | **The title says the point** | Write the title as an insight headline (see [section 2](#2-insight-headlines-8-before-and-after-examples)). | Read only the titles. Do they tell the story? |
| 3 | **Clear margins** | Keep about 0.5 inch (1.25 cm) empty on every edge. Nothing touches the edge of the slide except a full-bleed photo. | Is any text closer to the edge than the rest? |
| 4 | **Line things up** | Use the same left edge and the same title position on every slide. Use guides and the Align tools. | Flip between slides. Does the title jump? |
| 5 | **Big enough text** | Titles 28 to 40 pt. Body text 18 pt or more. Source notes 10 to 12 pt. A deck people read on their own (not presented) can go down to 14 pt body text. | Can you read it on a phone screen? |
| 6 | **Two fonts at most** | One for titles, one for body text, or one font in two weights. Keep the same sizes on every slide. | Count the fonts in the deck. |
| 7 | **Strong contrast** | Dark text on a light background, or light text on a dark one. Never place text over a busy photo without a solid box behind it. For web accessibility, a ratio of at least 4.5:1 is the common target for normal text. | Test your text colours in any free contrast checker. |
| 8 | **Colour with a purpose** | One main colour, one accent colour, plus greys. Use the accent only for the thing that matters. Do not show meaning with colour alone, for example red vs green. | Does the accent point at the key number? |
| 9 | **Less text** | At most 5 bullets, each one line long. Move detail to the speaker notes or an appendix. | Would you read it out loud word for word? Cut it. |
| 10 | **Clean images** | Use sharp, high-quality images. Hold Shift while resizing so they do not stretch. Keep one photo style through the deck. | Zoom to 200%. Is it blurry or stretched? |
| 11 | **Source notes** | Every number, chart or quote gets a source and a date in small text at the bottom of the slide. | Pick any number. Can you see where it came from? |
| 12 | **Page numbers** | Add slide numbers so people can say "on slide 7" in a meeting or an email. Hide them on the title slide. | Is there a number in the same corner of every slide? |

### Where to find the tools
| Task | PowerPoint | Google Slides |
|---|---|---|
| Show guides | View > Guides | View > Guides > Show guides |
| Align and space objects | Select objects, then Shape Format > Align | Select objects, then Arrange > Align or Distribute |
| Same fonts and colours everywhere | View > Slide Master | Slide > Edit theme |
| Slide numbers | Insert > Header & Footer > Slide number (tick "Don't show on title slide") | Insert > Slide numbers (tick "Skip title slides") |
| Speaker notes | View > Notes | View > Show speaker notes |

### Quick checklist (copy this)
```
[ ] One idea per slide
[ ] Every title says the point
[ ] About 0.5 inch (1.25 cm) margins on every edge
[ ] Titles and text line up on every slide
[ ] Titles 28 to 40 pt, body 18 pt or more (14 pt for read-only decks)
[ ] Two fonts at most
[ ] Strong contrast, no text on busy photos
[ ] One main colour, one accent, greys
[ ] 5 bullets at most, one line each
[ ] Sharp images, not stretched
[ ] Source and date under every number
[ ] Slide numbers on every slide except the title
```

### Common mistakes
- **Fixing design slide by slide.** Set fonts and colours once in the Slide Master or theme. Then every new slide matches.
- **Shrinking text to fit.** If text does not fit at 18 pt, the slide has too much on it. Cut or split.
- **Light grey text on white.** It looks clean on your screen and disappears on a projector.
- **Logos on every slide** that take space from the content. Keep it on the title and the last slide.
- **Animations on every bullet.** Use them only when the order of the reveal helps the story.
- **No slide numbers,** so nobody can point to the slide they have a question about.

## 5. Before you send your deck: 15-point checklist

A good deck can still fail at the last step: a wrong name, a broken link, a file too big to open on a phone. Spend 15 minutes on this list before you press send. It works for investor decks, sales decks and work presentations, in PowerPoint and Google Slides.

### The 15 points
**Content: what the deck says**

| # | Check | How to do it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The ask is clear** | The last slide says what you want (money, a meeting, a yes) and what happens next. |
| 2 | **Every title says the point** | Read only the titles, in order. Do they tell the story? (see [section 2](#2-insight-headlines-8-before-and-after-examples)) |
| 3 | **Every number is checked** | Each number is correct, the same on every slide and in your email, and has a source and a date. |
| 4 | **No spelling mistakes** | PowerPoint: Review > Spelling. Google Slides: Tools > Spelling > Spell check. Then read every slide once, slowly. |
| 5 | **No leftovers** | Press Ctrl+F (Cmd+F on Mac) and search for `[`, `TBD`, `XX`, `draft` and the name of the last company you sent a deck to. |

**File: what they receive**

| # | Check | How to do it |
|---|---|---|
| 6 | **Saved as a PDF** | PowerPoint: File > Export > Create PDF/XPS. Google Slides: File > Download > PDF document. Send the editable file only if they ask for it. |
| 7 | **The PDF looks right** | Open the PDF itself, not your slides. Check fonts, charts, text that is cut off and the number of pages. |
| 8 | **Nothing private inside** | Remove comments, speaker notes and hidden slides you do not want them to see. PowerPoint: File > Info > Check for Issues > Inspect Document. |
| 9 | **Small file** | Aim for under about 10 MB so it sends and opens fast. PowerPoint: select a picture, then Picture Format > Compress Pictures. |
| 10 | **A clear file name** | `Company-deck-2026-10.pdf`, not `final-v3-NEW.pdf`. |
| 11 | **Every link works** | Click each link in the PDF, not in your slides. |

**Sending: how it reaches them**

| # | Check | How to do it |
|---|---|---|
| 12 | **Checked on a phone** | Send the PDF to yourself and open it on your phone. Many people read decks there first. |
| 13 | **Shared links are view only** | Google Slides: Share > General access > "Anyone with the link" > Viewer. Then open the link in a private browser window to check it works when logged out. |
| 14 | **The right person, spelled right** | Check their name, their company name and their email address. |
| 15 | **A short email** | 3 to 5 lines: who you are, what you do, one proof point, your ask. A clear subject line. |

### Demo email (made-up company Tidepost)
Fill the [brackets] with your own real details.
```
Subject: Tidepost deck: online booking for boat tour operators

Hi [Name],

Tidepost lets small boat tour operators take bookings and payments online, day or night.
We have [X] paying operators as of [date].
We are raising [amount] to reach [milestone], and I would value 20 minutes of your time.

The deck is attached (10 slides, PDF).

[Your name]
[Company], [role]
```

### Quick checklist (copy this)
```
CONTENT
[ ] 1. The ask is clear on the last slide
[ ] 2. Titles alone tell the story
[ ] 3. Numbers checked, same everywhere, source and date
[ ] 4. Spell check run and every slide read once
[ ] 5. No leftovers: [ , TBD, XX, draft, old company names
FILE
[ ] 6. Saved as a PDF
[ ] 7. Opened the PDF: fonts, charts, page count
[ ] 8. No private comments, notes or hidden slides
[ ] 9. Under about 10 MB
[ ] 10. Clear file name with company and date
[ ] 11. Every link clicked in the PDF
SENDING
[ ] 12. Opened on a phone
[ ] 13. Shared link is view only and works when logged out
[ ] 14. Right name, company and email address
[ ] 15. Short email with a clear subject line
```

### Common mistakes
- **Another company's name** left in from the last deck you sent. Always search for it.
- **Checking the slides, not the PDF.** Fonts and charts can change when you export. Look at the file they will get.
- **A shared link that asks for access.** The reader clicks, sees "You need access" and moves on.
- **"Here is our deck" and nothing else.** Give one reason to open it in the email itself.
- **Sending at once after a big edit.** Wait 10 minutes, then read it again with fresh eyes.
- **Numbers in the email that do not match the deck.** Copy them from the same place.

## 6. Alignment shortcuts: 10 tips for PowerPoint and Google Slides

A slide where things are a few pixels off looks rushed, even if the reader cannot say why. Lining things up by eye is slow. These 10 tips do it for you in seconds.

Shortcuts are for Windows. On a Mac, use **Cmd** for Ctrl and **Option** for Alt. A few PowerPoint keys differ on Mac, so hover over a button to see its shortcut.

**How to select more than one object:** hold **Shift** and click each one, or drag a box around them. **Ctrl+A** selects everything on the slide.

### The 10 tips
| # | Tip | PowerPoint | Google Slides |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Line up several objects at once** | Select them, then Shape Format (or Picture Format) > Align > Left, Center, Right, Top, Middle or Bottom. Keyboard: press **Alt**, then **H, G, A**, then the letter shown on screen. | Select them, then **Arrange > Align** > Left, Center, Right, Top, Middle or Bottom. |
| 2 | **Make the gaps equal** | Select 3 or more objects, then Align > **Distribute Horizontally** or **Distribute Vertically**. | Select 3 or more objects, then **Arrange > Distribute** > Horizontally or Vertically. |
| 3 | **Center on the slide** | Align > tick **Align to Slide**, then Align Center (side to side) or Align Middle (top to bottom). Switch back to **Align Selected Objects** after. | **Arrange > Center on page** > Horizontally or Vertically. |
| 4 | **Move in a straight line** | Hold **Shift** while you drag. | Hold **Shift** while you drag. |
| 5 | **Nudge in small steps** | **Arrow keys** move a little. **Ctrl + arrow** moves a tiny step. | **Arrow keys** move a little. **Shift + arrow** moves 1 pixel. |
| 6 | **Copy with the same gap** | **Ctrl+D** to copy, move the copy where you want it, then **Ctrl+D** again: each new copy keeps the same gap. **Ctrl+Shift + drag** copies in a straight line. | **Ctrl+D** to copy. Place the copies, then use tip 2 to make the gaps equal. |
| 7 | **Use guides** | **Alt+F9** shows or hides guides. **Shift+F9** shows or hides the grid. Hold **Ctrl** and drag a guide to add another one. | **View > Guides > Show guides**. Use **Edit guides** to type exact positions. |
| 8 | **Same spot on every slide** | Right-click > **Size and Position**, then type the same horizontal and vertical position for each title or logo. | Right-click > **Format options > Position**, then type the same X and Y for each title or logo. |
| 9 | **Group so things move together** | **Ctrl+G** groups. **Ctrl+Shift+G** ungroups. | **Ctrl+Alt+G** groups. **Ctrl+Alt+Shift+G** ungroups. |
| 10 | **Resize without stretching** | Hold **Shift** and drag a corner. Hold **Ctrl+Shift** to resize from the middle. | Hold **Shift** and drag a corner. |

### Two extras that save time
- **Snapping gets in the way?** PowerPoint: hold **Alt** while you drag to place an object freely. Google Slides: **View > Snap to** and turn off Grid or Guides.
- **Forgot a shortcut?** PowerPoint: press **Alt** and the letters for each tab and button appear. Google Slides: press **Ctrl+/** to see the full shortcut list.

### Demo: line up a "How it works" slide in under a minute
Made-up company Tidepost has 3 steps on one slide: an icon and a short label for each.

1. Select icon 1 and its label. **Align Center**, then **group** them. Repeat for steps 2 and 3.
2. Select the 3 groups. **Align Top** so they sit on one line.
3. Keep them selected. **Distribute Horizontally** so the gaps are equal.
4. **Group** all 3, then **center** the group on the slide (tip 3).
5. Flip to the next slide and back. If nothing jumps, you are done.

### Quick checklist (copy this)
```
[ ] Objects in a row share the same top or middle line
[ ] Gaps between objects are equal (Distribute)
[ ] Main content is centered or sits on the same left edge
[ ] Titles are in the same spot on every slide (same X and Y)
[ ] Icons and their labels are grouped
[ ] No stretched images (resized with Shift)
[ ] Nothing touches the slide edge (about 0.5 inch / 1.25 cm margin)
```

### Common mistakes
- **Lining up by eye.** It looks fine at 60% zoom and crooked on a big screen. Use Align and Distribute.
- **Aligning to the slide by accident** in PowerPoint. If objects jump to the edge, switch back to "Align Selected Objects".
- **Distributing only 2 objects.** You need 3 or more for equal gaps.
- **Moving a title on one slide only.** Change it in the Slide Master (PowerPoint) or Edit theme (Google Slides) so every slide matches.
- **Ungrouped icons and labels** that drift apart when you move them.

## 7. Speaker notes that help: what to write under each slide

Speaker notes are the private text under each slide. You see them while you present. Your audience does not. Good notes keep you calm and on time. Bad notes turn into a script you read out loud, word for word, while people stop listening.

The rule: **notes are prompts, not a script.** You should be able to glance down for 2 seconds and know what to say next.

### What to write under each slide (5 parts)
| # | Part | What to write | Example (Tidepost demo) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The point** | One short sentence: what this slide proves. Say it in your own words, not the title again. | Operators lose bookings after 6 pm. |
| 2 | **2 or 3 talking points** | Key words, not full sentences. One line each. | Phone only. Voicemail at night. Customers book elsewhere. |
| 3 | **The exact number** | Any number you will say, with its source and date, so you never guess under pressure. | [X] of [Y] operators we spoke to, [month year] interviews. |
| 4 | **The bridge** | One line that leads to the next slide. | "So we built a way to book at any hour." |
| 5 | **Cues** | Short reminders in brackets: when to click, pause, show something or check the time. | [CLICK: show chart] [PAUSE] [2:30] |

**Optional 6th part:** one question people often ask about this slide, with a one-line answer. It helps most on the market, traction and ask slides.

### 3 before and after examples
Made-up company Tidepost (a booking app for small boat tour operators). Fill the [brackets] with your own real numbers.

**Example 1: Problem slide**

Before (a script nobody can read at a glance):
```
Good morning everyone, thank you for having us. So today I want to start by talking
about the problem we saw when we first met boat tour operators. Most of them are very
small businesses and they take bookings over the phone, which means that when they are
out on the water or when it is late at night they cannot answer, and this means that...
```

After (prompts you can read in 2 seconds):
```
POINT: Operators lose bookings when they cannot pick up the phone.
- Out on the water all day
- After 6 pm: voicemail only
- Customer books another tour instead
NUMBER: [X] of [Y] operators we interviewed, [month year]
BRIDGE: "So we built a way to book at any hour."
[CLICK]
```

**Example 2: Traction slide**

Before (too vague to help under pressure):
```
Talk about numbers. Growth is good. Mention the pilots.
```

After (exact facts, ready for questions):
```
POINT: Operators pay for it and stay.
- [X] paying operators as of [date]
- Up from [Y] in [month]
- [Z] pilots in [region], started [month]
SOURCE: our billing data, [date]
IF ASKED "Why did growth slow in [month]?": [one-line honest answer]
BRIDGE: "Here is how we make money from each operator."
[TIME CHECK: should be at 5:00]
```

**Example 3: The ask slide**

Before (no number, no stop):
```
Ask for the money. Say thank you. Maybe mention the timeline.
```

After (clear ask, then stop talking):
```
POINT: Raising [amount] to reach [milestone] by [date].

- **Sending notes you meant to keep private.** Check them before you send any editable file.

## 8. One-page company overview: 6 blocks for sales and partner meetings

A one-page overview tells a buyer or a partner who you are, what you do for them and what to do next, on a single page. Send it before a meeting so they come prepared, or leave it behind after, so they can share it with their team.

### The 6 blocks
| # | Block | The question it answers | What to put | Size |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Who we are** | What is this company, in one line? | Name, logo and one sentence: "We help [who] [do what] so they [result]." | 1 sentence |
| 2 | **The problem** | What pain do you fix, and for whom? | One type of customer, their pain and how they handle it today. | 2 to 3 sentences |
| 3 | **What we offer** | What do I get? | 3 benefits as short bullets, then how it works in 3 steps. | 3 bullets + 3 steps |
| 4 | **Proof** | Why should I believe you? | Real numbers with a date, pilots, partners or a customer quote you have permission to use. | 2 to 3 lines |
| 5 | **Why us** | Why you and not the usual way? | 2 or 3 points that make you different from what they use today. | 2 to 3 bullets |
| 6 | **Next step** | What do you want me to do? | One clear action (a demo, a trial, an intro call) and who to contact. | 1 line + contact |

**Tip:** if you have no proof yet, use block 4 for what is ready today: a working product, a short demo video or a pilot that starts on a set date. Never fill it with guesses.

## 9. Turn a report into slides: 5 steps from a long document to a 6-slide summary

You have a 20-page report and a 15-minute meeting. Nobody will read the report in the room, and a deck with 20 pasted pages will lose them by slide 3. This guide turns any long document (a quarterly report, a research paper, a project review) into **6 slides that people remember**, plus an appendix for the detail.

The idea: **the report holds everything, the slides hold the message.** Your job is to choose, not to copy.

### The 5 steps
| # | Step | What to do | Done when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Write the one message** | Before you open slides, finish this sentence: "In [period], [what happened], so we [action or ask]." | You can say it out loud in 15 seconds. |
| 2 | **Make a fact list** | Read the report once with the message in mind. Copy only the facts that support it or put it at risk. Write the page number and the date next to each one. | You have 6 to 12 facts, each with a page number. |
| 3 | **Sort facts into 6 slides** | Put each fact into one of the 6 slides below. A fact that fits nowhere goes to the appendix or stays in the report. | Every slide has 1 to 3 facts. No slide is empty. |
| 4 | **Write headlines and pick one visual** | Give each slide an insight headline (see [section 2](#2-insight-headlines-8-before-and-after-examples)). Then pick one chart, table or short list that proves it (see [section 3](#3-chart-chooser-which-chart-for-which-message)). | Reading only the 6 headlines tells the whole story. |
| 5 | **Check numbers, then build the appendix** | Check every number on the slides against the report. Put the page number in the speaker notes. Move the detail people may ask about into appendix slides after the last slide. | Someone who has not read the report can tell you the message after the meeting. |

**Tip:** do steps 1 to 3 on paper or in a plain text file, not in PowerPoint or Google Slides. Slides make you think about layout too early.

### The 6 slides
| # | Slide | The question it answers | What to show |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Title with the message** | What is this about, in one line? | Your one message from step 1 as the title. Date, team and who is presenting. |
| 2 | **Summary in 3 points** | If I only see one slide, what do I need to know? | 3 short lines: the main result, the main problem, the decision or ask. |
| 3 | **What went well** | What worked, and why? | 1 to 3 wins with real numbers and the reason behind each one. |
| 4 | **What did not go well** | What went wrong or is at risk? | 1 to 3 problems, what caused them and what is being done. Be honest. |
| 5 | **The key numbers** | How do the numbers look, compared with before? | One table or chart with 4 to 6 numbers: this period, last period, change. |
| 6 | **Decisions and next steps** | What do you need from us, and what happens next? | The decision you need today, then 3 next steps, each with an owner and a date. |

**Short on time?** Drop slide 5 and put the 2 most important numbers on slides 3 and 4. Never drop slide 6.

```

### Common mistakes
- **Following the report's chapters.** The report is ordered for reading. Slides are ordered for deciding. Start with the message, not chapter 1.
- **Copying paragraphs onto slides.** If you can read it aloud for 30 seconds, it is too long. Cut to a headline and 3 lines.
- **Keeping every chart.** One chart per slide, the one that proves the headline. The rest go to the appendix.
- **Hiding the problem.** People find it later and stop trusting the whole deck. Put it on slide 4 with the fix.
- **No ask at the end.** A summary with no decision ends in "thanks, interesting". Name what you need.
- **Numbers that differ from the report.** One wrong number makes people doubt the rest. Check each one against its page.

## 10. Pitch deck numbers slide: which 4 to 6 numbers to show and how to label them

The numbers slide (often called "traction" or "key metrics") is where investors slow down and read closely. A slide with 9 numbers, no dates and unclear units makes them doubt all of them. A slide with 4 to 6 clear, dated numbers is easy to trust and easy to check.

The rule: **4 to 6 numbers, each with a unit, a date and a source.** Everything else goes to the appendix.

### Step 1: pick 4 to 6 numbers for your stage
| Your stage | Pick from these | Leave off the slide |
|---|---|---|
| **Before revenue** | Waitlist sign-ups, signed pilots or letters of intent, customers interviewed, active users in a pilot | Forecasts shown as if they were results |
| **Early revenue** | Paying customers, monthly recurring revenue (MRR) or monthly revenue, monthly growth, retention | Downloads, page views or followers on their own |
| **Growing** | Revenue or annual recurring revenue (ARR), growth, gross margin, cost to win one customer, months to earn that cost back | Numbers that do not help a decision, like press mentions |

**How to choose:** pick the numbers that prove people **want it**, **pay for it** and **stay**. If you only have 3 strong numbers, show 3. Never add weak numbers to fill space.

### Step 2: know what each number means
Investors will ask how you counted. Use the plain meaning below and say it the same way every time.

| Number | Plain meaning | Label it like this (Tidepost demo) |
|---|---|---|
| **MRR** | Money from subscriptions you get every month. No one-off fees. | €[X] MRR, September 2026 |
| **ARR** | MRR x 12. Only for subscription income. | €[X] ARR (MRR x 12), September 2026 |
| **Monthly growth** | How much a number changed from one month to the next, in %. | Revenue up [X]% a month on average, June to September 2026 |
| **Retention** | Share of customers who still pay after a set time. | [X]% of operators who joined in Q1 2026 still pay 6 months later |
| **Gross margin** | Share of revenue left after the direct cost of serving customers. | [X]% gross margin, Q3 2026 |
| **Cost to win a customer (CAC)** | Sales and marketing spend divided by new customers in the same period. | €[X] to win one operator, Q3 2026 |
| **Payback** | Months a customer needs to pay back what it cost to win them. | CAC paid back in [X] months |
| **Booking value (GMV)** | Total value of sales through your platform. **Not your revenue.** | €[X] booked through Tidepost in Q3 2026, Tidepost keeps [Y]% |
| **Active users** | People who did one key action in a set period. Name the action. | [X] operators took at least one booking in September 2026 |

### Step 3: label every number (5 parts)
```
[number]  [unit]  [what it counts]            [date or period]   + source in the footnote
€[X]      MRR     from [Y] paying operators,  September 2026
```

7 labelling rules:
1. **Put a date on every number.** "As of 30 Sep 2026" for a count, "September 2026" or "Q3 2026" for a period.
2. **Say what a % is a change from.** Not "up 40%" but "up [X]% from June to September 2026".
3. **Say the currency and use one currency** across the whole deck.
4. **Pick one style for big numbers** and keep it: €12k or €12,000, not both on one slide.
5. **Round down, not up.** 1,940 operators is "1,900+", never "2,000".
6. **Mark totals clearly.** "Total since launch" and "in September" are very different. Never mix them without a label.
7. **Add a source line** in small text at the bottom: where the numbers come from and when you pulled them.

### Step 4: lay out the slide
- **Headline says the point**, not "Traction" (see [section 2](#2-insight-headlines-8-before-and-after-examples)).
- **4 to 6 number tiles** in one row or a 2 x 2 or 2 x 3 grid, all the same size.
- **Inside each tile:** the big number on top (40 to 54 pt), a short label under it (16 to 18 pt), the date in the label.
- **One chart at most.** A column chart of one number per month shows growth best (see [section 3](#3-chart-chooser-which-chart-for-which-message)). Start the axis at zero.
- **Footnote** at the bottom (10 to 12 pt): source, date pulled, and how you counted anything unusual.
- **Your strongest number goes first**, top left, where people look first.

### Common mistakes
- **Showing every number you have.** More numbers do not look stronger. They look like you are hiding the weak one.
- **No date.** "[X] customers" with no date could be from last year. Investors will assume the worst.
- **Growth % on a tiny start.** Going from 2 to 8 is 300%. Show the real numbers next to the %.
- **Mixing up booking value and revenue.** Name each one correctly. Getting caught here costs trust fast.
- **Forecasts on the traction slide.** Future numbers belong on the plan or ask slide, clearly marked as a plan.
