## 1. The 10-slide investor deck outline

Investors see a lot of decks and spend only a few minutes on each one. This order answers their questions in the order they ask them. Each slide has **one job**.

| # | Slide | The question it must answer | What to show |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Title** | What is this company, in one line? | Name, logo and one sentence: what you do and for whom. |
| 2 | **Problem** | What pain exists, and who has it? | One clear customer, their pain, how they handle it today and why that falls short. |
| 3 | **Solution** | How do you fix that pain? | What you offer, in plain words. The main benefit, not every feature. |
| 4 | **Product** | What does it look like and how does it work? | Screenshots or photos and the 3 steps a customer goes through. |
| 5 | **Market** | How big can this get? | Who buys, how many of them there are and what they spend. Show your maths. |
| 6 | **Traction** | Is there proof people want it? | Real numbers only: users, revenue, growth, pilots, waitlist. Put a date on every number. |
| 7 | **Business model** | How do you make money? | Price, who pays and how often. Cost to win a customer and what one customer is worth, if you know them. |
| 8 | **Competition** | Who else solves this, and why will you win? | A simple table or a 2x2 chart. Name real options, including "they use a spreadsheet" or "they do nothing". |
| 9 | **Team** | Why are you the right people? | Founders with the one fact that matters for each, plus key hires or advisors. |
| 10 | **The ask** | What do you want, and what will it get done? | The amount you are raising, what it pays for (3 or 4 parts) and the milestone it gets you to. |

**Tip:** if traction is your strongest point, move it up to slide 3 or 4. Lead with your best proof.

### Headlines that say the point
Every slide title should say the point, not just the topic. The reader should get the story from the titles alone.

| Slide | Weak title | Strong title (Tidepost demo) |
|---|---|---|
| Problem | Problem | Small tour operators take bookings by phone and miss customers after hours |
| Solution | Our solution | Tidepost lets customers book and pay for a boat tour in 2 minutes, day or night |
| Market | Market size | [X] small tour operators in our first 3 countries, each paying [Y] a year for booking tools |
| Traction | Traction | [X] paying operators, up from [Y] in [month] |
| The ask | Investment | Raising [amount] to reach [milestone] in [number] months |

Fill the [brackets] with your own real numbers. Never round up or guess.

### Rules for the whole deck
1. **One idea per slide.** If a slide has two ideas, split it.
2. **10 slides in the main deck.** Put extra detail in an appendix at the end.
3. **Big text.** If it is hard to read on a phone screen, it is too small.
4. **A source and a date on every number.** Small text at the bottom of the slide is fine.
5. **Same fonts and colours on every slide.** Two fonts at most.
6. **Send it as a PDF** with a clear file name, for example `Tidepost-deck-2026-10.pdf`.

### Common mistakes
- **A problem with no person in it.** "The tourism market is broken" says nothing. Name who hurts and how.
- **Market size from the top down.** "If we get 1% of a huge market" is not a plan. Count real buyers instead.
- **A feature list on the solution slide.** Show the one main benefit first.
- **"We have no competitors."** There is always another option, even if it is a spreadsheet.
- **An ask with no number** or no milestone.
- **Too much text.** If you would read it out loud word for word, cut it.

### Fill-in outline (copy this)
```
1. Title:          [Company] helps [customer] [do what]
2. Problem:        [Customer] struggles with [pain]. Today they [workaround], which [why it falls short].
3. Solution:       [Company] lets them [main benefit].
4. Product:        Step 1 [ ], Step 2 [ ], Step 3 [ ]. Add screenshots.
5. Market:         [Number] buyers x [yearly spend] = [market size]. Source: [ ]
6. Traction:       [Real number] [users / revenue / pilots] as of [date].
7. Business model: [Price] per [month / order / user], paid by [who].
8. Competition:    [Option A], [Option B], [doing nothing]. We win because [reason].
9. Team:           [Name], [role], [one fact that matters]. Repeat for each founder.
10. The ask:       Raising [amount] for [use 1], [use 2], [use 3] to reach [milestone] by [date].
```

## 2. Insight headlines: 8 before and after examples

A **topic title** names the subject: "Sales update". An **insight headline** says the point as a full sentence: "Online sales grew in May, mostly from returning customers".

People often read only the titles. Insight headlines let them get your message even if they skip the rest of the slide.

### How to write one in 4 steps
1. Look at the slide and ask: **"So what?"**
2. Write the answer as **one full sentence with a verb**.
3. Add the **number or the reason**, if you have one.
4. Keep it **short**: 2 lines at most, about 15 words.

### 8 before and after examples
These are generic demo topics. Fill the [brackets] with your own real numbers.

| # | Before (topic title) | After (insight headline) | Why it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sales update | Online sales grew [X]% in [month], mostly from returning customers | Gives the result and the reason |
| 2 | Customer survey results | Customers like the product but say delivery is too slow | States the main finding, not just "results" |
| 3 | Website traffic | Most visitors come from phones, so we fix the mobile page first | Links the data to an action |
| 4 | Hiring plan | We need 2 support staff by [month] to answer customers within [X] hours | Says what, when and why |
| 5 | Budget | Moving [amount] from ads to email keeps costs flat and reaches past buyers | Shows the trade-off |
| 6 | Product roadmap | Online booking ships in [month]; payments follow in [month] | Gives the order and the dates |
| 7 | Competitor review | Other tools win on price; we win on how fast a new user can start | Makes the difference clear |
| 8 | Project status | The launch is on track, but testing is 1 week behind | Honest status that flags the risk |

### Fill-in patterns (copy these)
```
[Thing] went [up / down] by [amount] in [period] because [reason]
[Finding], so we should [action]
[Option A] beats [Option B] on [what matters most]
[Project] is [on track / late] because [reason]; next step is [action]
We need [what] by [date] to [result]
```

### The 30-second test
Read **only the titles** of your deck, in order, out loud. If they tell the whole story, your headlines work. If they sound like a list of topics, rewrite them.

### Common mistakes
- **One-word titles** like "Overview", "Results", "Analysis" or "Q3". They name a topic but say nothing.
- **A headline the slide does not prove.** If the chart shows something else, change the chart or the headline.
- **Too long.** Three lines is a paragraph, not a headline. Move detail into the slide.
- **Vague words** like "strong", "good" or "significant" when you have a real number. Use the number.
- **A question as a title** ("Why are sales down?"). Give the answer instead.

## 3. Chart chooser: which chart for which message

Pick the chart from your **message**, not from your data. First write the insight headline (see [section 2](#2-insight-headlines-8-before-and-after-examples)). Then pick the chart that proves it.

### The cheat sheet (8 rows)
Demo headlines use the made-up company Tidepost. Fill the [brackets] with your own real numbers.

| # | Your message is about | Example headline | Best chart | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Change over time** | Bookings grew every month since [month] | **Line chart.** Columns also work for up to about 8 time periods. | Pie chart |
| 2 | **Ranking or comparing items** | Sunset tours sell more than any other tour | **Bar chart**, sorted biggest to smallest. Use flat (horizontal) bars when the labels are long. | 3D bars, random order |
| 3 | **Parts of a whole** | Half of all bookings now come from phones | **One stacked bar**, or a pie or donut with 2 to 4 slices | A pie with many thin slices |
| 4 | **How a total builds up or breaks down** | Fuel and crew take [X]% of each ticket price | **Waterfall chart** | Several separate pies |
| 5 | **Plan vs actual, or before vs after** | Costs came in [X]% under plan | **Two bars side by side** (clustered bar) for each item | Two separate charts the reader must compare |
| 6 | **How two numbers move together** | Longer tours get higher review scores | **Scatter plot** (one dot per item) | A line joining dots that are not in time order |
| 7 | **Spread or range** | Most orders are between [X] and [Y] | **Histogram** (columns for each range, such as 0 to 20, 20 to 40) | Showing only the average |
| 8 | **One big number** | [X] paying operators as of [date] | **No chart.** Show the number large, with a short label and the date. | A chart with one bar |

### 7 rules that make any chart easier to read
1. **The headline says the point.** "Bookings grew every month", not "Bookings 2026".
2. **Highlight one thing.** Put the bar or line that matters in your brand colour. Make the rest grey.
3. **Label the data directly.** Put names next to lines and bars, so the reader does not need a legend.
4. **Bar and column charts start at zero.** Cutting the axis makes small changes look big.
5. **Remove clutter:** 3D effects, shadows, heavy gridlines, extra decimals.
6. **Round numbers.** Write 1.2M, not 1,203,456, unless the exact figure matters.
7. **Add a source and a date** in small text under the chart.

### Where to find each chart
| Chart | PowerPoint | Google Slides |
|---|---|---|
| Line, bar, column, pie | Insert > Chart | Insert > Chart |
| Stacked bar, clustered bar | Insert > Chart > Bar, then pick the stacked or clustered style | Insert > Chart > Bar, then open the linked Sheet and change the chart type |
| Scatter, histogram, waterfall | Insert > Chart (scatter is called X Y; histogram and waterfall need PowerPoint 2016 or newer) | Make it in Google Sheets (Insert > Chart > Chart type), then Insert > Chart > From Sheets |

### The 5-second test
Show the slide to someone for 5 seconds, then hide it. Ask: "What was the point?" If they cannot say it, cut detail or change the chart.

### Common mistakes
- **Choosing the chart first** and the message later.
- **Pie charts for change over time.** A pie shows one moment, not a trend.
- **Too many lines** on one chart. More than 4 and nobody can follow them. Highlight one, grey the rest, or split the chart.
- **Different colours for the same thing** on different slides. Keep "this year" the same colour everywhere.
- **A chart that does not match the headline.** If the headline says "costs fell", the chart must show costs falling.

## 4. Slide design checklist: 12 rules

Good design is not decoration. It helps people read the slide fast and trust it. These 12 rules work in PowerPoint and Google Slides, for any deck.

### The 12 rules
| # | Rule | What to do | Quick check |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **One idea per slide** | If a slide makes two points, split it into two slides. | Can you say the point in one sentence? |
| 2 | **The title says the point** | Write the title as an insight headline (see [section 2](#2-insight-headlines-8-before-and-after-examples)). | Read only the titles. Do they tell the story? |
| 3 | **Clear margins** | Keep about 0.5 inch (1.25 cm) empty on every edge. Nothing touches the edge of the slide except a full-bleed photo. | Is any text closer to the edge than the rest? |
| 4 | **Line things up** | Use the same left edge and the same title position on every slide. Use guides and the Align tools. | Flip between slides. Does the title jump? |
| 5 | **Big enough text** | Titles 28 to 40 pt. Body text 18 pt or more. Source notes 10 to 12 pt. A deck people read on their own (not presented) can go down to 14 pt body text. | Can you read it on a phone screen? |
| 6 | **Two fonts at most** | One for titles, one for body text, or one font in two weights. Keep the same sizes on every slide. | Count the fonts in the deck. |
| 7 | **Strong contrast** | Dark text on a light background, or light text on a dark one. Never place text over a busy photo without a solid box behind it. For web accessibility, a ratio of at least 4.5:1 is the common target for normal text. | Test your text colours in any free contrast checker. |
| 8 | **Colour with a purpose** | One main colour, one accent colour, plus greys. Use the accent only for the thing that matters. Do not show meaning with colour alone, for example red vs green. | Does the accent point at the key number? |
| 9 | **Less text** | At most 5 bullets, each one line long. Move detail to the speaker notes or an appendix. | Would you read it out loud word for word? Cut it. |
| 10 | **Clean images** | Use sharp, high-quality images. Hold Shift while resizing so they do not stretch. Keep one photo style through the deck. | Zoom to 200%. Is it blurry or stretched? |
| 11 | **Source notes** | Every number, chart or quote gets a source and a date in small text at the bottom of the slide. | Pick any number. Can you see where it came from? |
| 12 | **Page numbers** | Add slide numbers so people can say "on slide 7" in a meeting or an email. Hide them on the title slide. | Is there a number in the same corner of every slide? |

### Where to find the tools
| Task | PowerPoint | Google Slides |
|---|---|---|
| Show guides | View > Guides | View > Guides > Show guides |
| Align and space objects | Select objects, then Shape Format > Align | Select objects, then Arrange > Align or Distribute |
| Same fonts and colours everywhere | View > Slide Master | Slide > Edit theme |
| Slide numbers | Insert > Header & Footer > Slide number (tick "Don't show on title slide") | Insert > Slide numbers (tick "Skip title slides") |
| Speaker notes | View > Notes | View > Show speaker notes |

### Quick checklist (copy this)
```
[ ] One idea per slide
[ ] Every title says the point
[ ] About 0.5 inch (1.25 cm) margins on every edge
[ ] Titles and text line up on every slide
[ ] Titles 28 to 40 pt, body 18 pt or more (14 pt for read-only decks)
[ ] Two fonts at most
[ ] Strong contrast, no text on busy photos
[ ] One main colour, one accent, greys
[ ] 5 bullets at most, one line each
[ ] Sharp images, not stretched
[ ] Source and date under every number
[ ] Slide numbers on every slide except the title
```

### Common mistakes
- **Fixing design slide by slide.** Set fonts and colours once in the Slide Master or theme. Then every new slide matches.
- **Shrinking text to fit.** If text does not fit at 18 pt, the slide has too much on it. Cut or split.
- **Light grey text on white.** It looks clean on your screen and disappears on a projector.
- **Logos on every slide** that take space from the content. Keep it on the title and the last slide.
- **Animations on every bullet.** Use them only when the order of the reveal helps the story.
- **No slide numbers,** so nobody can point to the slide they have a question about.
